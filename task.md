## Finance

**Domain / specialty:** Finance: corporate credit analysis, lease accounting, financial covenant compliance

### Task details

**Situation.** You are the Senior Director of Corporate Finance at Solaire Cold Chain Logistics, Inc., a private, sponsor-owned refrigerated warehousing and distribution company. Solaire's CFO has asked you to evaluate a proposed sale-leaseback of the company's Joliet, Illinois distribution center before she takes the deal to the board and to the administrative agent under Solaire's senior secured credit agreement.

**Recipient / work product.** Write a compliance memo to the CFO (two to three pages is sufficient) that reaches a supported conclusion on (1) how the transaction must be accounted for under U.S. GAAP, (2) whether it can close as structured under Solaire's existing credit agreement without lender consent, and (3) what would need to change if it cannot.

**Permitted resources.** Use only the three exhibits below. All financial and contractual figures are fictional and complete as given; no external research is needed to fill in a number. You may rely on your own knowledge of U.S. GAAP (FASB ASC 842) to identify and apply the relevant authoritative guidance, and should cite the specific paragraph(s) you rely on.

**Assumptions.** All dollar amounts are in USD millions unless noted otherwise. Solaire's fiscal year ends December 31. The most recent compliance certificate under the credit agreement is for the trailing twelve months ended June 30, 2026. Treat every figure in the exhibits as accurate and final.

### Prompt

1. Determine whether the proposed sale of the Joliet facility, combined with the accompanying leaseback described in Exhibit C, qualifies as a sale under ASC 842-40. State the specific provision(s) that govern your conclusion.
2. Compute Solaire's Consolidated EBITDA for the trailing twelve months ended June 30, 2026, as that term is defined in the credit agreement (Exhibit B), including the effect of any applicable limitation on addbacks.
3. Assuming the transaction closes on the terms in Exhibit C and the net proceeds are applied as described there, compute the Consolidated Net Leverage Ratio immediately following closing and state whether Solaire remains in compliance with the maximum leverage covenant.
4. Determine whether the transaction as sized can proceed under the credit agreement's Sale and Leaseback Transactions covenant without further lender consent. If it cannot, quantify the shortfall.
5. Recommend whether the deal can close as currently structured, and if not, identify what would need to change (deal size, structure, or lender consent).

Show the arithmetic behind every figure you report, not just the final numbers.

### Reference solution

**1. Sale accounting conclusion.** The transaction does not qualify for sale accounting under ASC 842-40. Under ASC 842-40-25-1, a transfer of an asset in a sale-leaseback is evaluated under the control-transfer requirements of ASC 606. ASC 842-40-25-3 provides that if the seller-lessee holds an option to repurchase the asset, that option precludes sale accounting unless (a) the option is exercisable only at the asset's then-prevailing fair value at the time of exercise, and (b) substantially similar alternative assets are readily available in the marketplace. Here, the repurchase option is exercisable at a fixed price of $41.6 million regardless of the property's fair value in year 15, so condition (a) is not met and the option precludes sale treatment on its own; the availability of alternative assets is not even reached. Under ASC 842-40-25-4, because the transfer does not qualify as a sale, Solaire (the seller-lessee) does not derecognize the Joliet facility and continues to depreciate it, and accounts for the amounts received from Meridian as a financial liability (a failed sale-leaseback financing obligation) rather than sale proceeds. No gain or loss is recognized at closing.

**2. Consolidated EBITDA.**
Unadjusted EBITDA = Net income $24.0 + interest $14.0 + tax $7.0 + D&A $9.0 = $54.0 million.
Addback (d), stock-based compensation, is not subject to the 15% cap: $2.0 million.
Addbacks (e) through (h) consist of restructuring $2.5, sponsor fees $1.0, transaction/advisory costs $4.0, and loss on disposal $1.5, totaling $9.0 million, and are subject to the cap.
Cap base per the definition = Consolidated EBITDA before the (e)-(h) addbacks but after the (d) addback = $54.0 + $2.0 = $56.0 million. Cap = 15% x $56.0 = $8.4 million.
Because $9.0 million of proposed (e)-(h) addbacks exceeds the $8.4 million cap, only $8.4 million is allowed; $0.6 million is disallowed.
Consolidated EBITDA = $54.0 + $2.0 + $8.4 = $64.4 million.

**3. Post-closing leverage.** Because the transaction is a financing rather than a sale, it has no effect on Consolidated Net Income or Consolidated EBITDA; $64.4 million applies both before and after closing.
Net cash proceeds = $52.0 million gross less $1.0 million closing costs = $51.0 million. Under Section 1.01's Indebtedness definition, this $51.0 million failed sale-leaseback financing obligation is Indebtedness (clause (iii)) even though it is not a loan.
Post-closing cash: $22.0 beginning + $52.0 gross proceeds - $1.0 closing costs - $15.0 revolver repayment - $5.0 other debt redemption = $53.0 million.
Post-closing Consolidated Total Debt: $180.0 Term Loan B (unchanged) + $51.0 financing obligation = $231.0 million (the revolver and other secured debt are retired and drop out).
Post-closing net debt = $231.0 - $53.0 = $178.0 million, which is identical to the pre-transaction net debt of $200.0 - $22.0 = $178.0 million. This is not a coincidence: whenever a failed sale-leaseback's financing obligation is recorded at net proceeds and 100% of those net proceeds either retire debt or sit in cash, the increase in gross debt and the increase in cash are equal, so net debt is unchanged.
Consolidated Net Leverage Ratio = $178.0 / $64.4 = 2.76x (both before and after closing), which is comfortably within the 4.25x maximum under Section 7.11. The leverage covenant does not block this transaction.

**4. Sale and Leaseback Transactions covenant.** Section 7.09 defines a Sale and Leaseback Transaction by reference to the underlying sale-and-rent-back arrangement itself, expressly "whether or not such transaction is characterized as a sale for accounting purposes." The GAAP conclusion in Question 1 therefore does not change the analysis under this covenant: the $52.0 million of aggregate consideration is tested against the $40.0 million basket regardless of the failed-sale characterization. $52.0 million exceeds the $40.0 million basket by $12.0 million, so the transaction as sized breaches Section 7.09 and cannot close without an amendment or waiver, notwithstanding that it passes the leverage covenant.

**5. Recommendation.** The leverage covenant is not an obstacle; on these facts the transaction is leverage-neutral because it is accounted for as a financing rather than a sale. The blocking issue is Section 7.09's $40.0 million sale-and-leaseback basket. Solaire cannot close the transaction as structured without either (a) obtaining the Administrative Agent's consent to a basket increase or a one-time waiver, or (b) restructuring the deal to bring aggregate consideration to $40.0 million or less, for example by selling a smaller interest in the facility or excluding an adjacent parcel from the transaction. Renegotiating the purchase option to a fair-value repurchase right would resolve the GAAP sale-accounting question but would not by itself cure the Section 7.09 basket breach, since that covenant applies regardless of accounting characterization.

**Acceptable alternatives.** A response that records the financing obligation at the gross $52.0 million (treating the $1.0 million of costs as a financing expense rather than a discount against the liability) is acceptable; it changes post-closing net debt to $179.0 million and the leverage ratio to approximately 2.78x, which does not change the compliance conclusion. A response that separately flags the year-15 repurchase option as also relevant to whether the leaseback itself would be a finance lease is acceptable context but is not required, since the repurchase option resolves the sale-accounting question on its own under ASC 842-40-25-3.
